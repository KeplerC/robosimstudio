**Label gallery port — round 2**

The two new path-traced 1080p takes reach 100% bonded, with centre errors below 1.3 mm and box excursions below 1.6 mm. Print orientation, framing, materials and frozen drift are corrected. The full audit also exposes a remaining pickup failure in its plain replay; that NOTE and the associated contact-buffer warnings are retained below.

This revision addresses the round-1 review. The catalog `yam_bimanual`, stock arm/gripper servos and geometry, free 0.5 kg 100 × 120 × 50 mm parcel, `rss.tapes.LABEL` material, rigid pull tab, original bench-relative layout, and nine-line straight-down pressing plan are retained. The robot commands only its arm cursor and fingers. The tape's own adhesive law makes the bonds; there is no attachment to the robot or scripted object motion.

**Print, framing and materials.** The parcel's top print no longer has the inappropriate vertical flip. Its native mesh UVs select the printed region of `assets/parcel_atlas.png`; the separate cloth texture retains the flip required by its own UV convention. The label's material coordinates are explicitly fixed to its 9 × 20 grid at renderer construction. This removes the general cloth renderer's first-frame PCA-axis ambiguity: opening a camera after the lay no longer rotates its print. Only UVs are written once on the native deforming mesh, not vertex positions or per-frame decals. The dashed target remains 88 × 50 mm. `run/review_macro_t0.png`, produced by `./ctl snapshot --camera macro` after the final reset and opened for inspection, shows PLACE LABEL HERE and RSS upright, reading left to right. `run/hero_initial.png` confirms the same first state in the final film. The macro camera is aimed farther toward the loose label and widened from 26° to 34°. Both film cameras are now 1920 × 1080. In the original world frame, the new macro eye is (0.90, −0.29, 0.94), looking at (0.60, −0.15, 0.78). The wide camera keeps its earlier view and 40° field of view.

Kraft's coarse mottling is much weaker and its base is lighter. All parcel faces use a common physical texture scale: one kraft tile represents 200 × 200 mm. **Rest diagnosis and initial conditions.** The original drift was predominantly planar sliding and rotation, not the tab falling over. `run/original_rest_trace.json` records tab pose/tilt and both sheet centroids. At 100 seconds, the tab had moved (+19.082, −5.620, −0.529) mm and turned −10.214°, while its tilt was only 0.043°. The sheet centroid moved 13.556 mm and the largest particle 20.892 mm. Robot-free comparisons reproduced it, excluding finger contact as the cause.

The label now starts from `assets/label_rest.npz`, a freely settled 200-second initial shape and tab pose. `PrintedLabel.build` applies these positions during construction, after the stock cloth establishes its original rest lengths, bend angles, masses and tab clamps. It never rewrites running state. The bake uses a finer numerical friction threshold to prepare the initial shape, then one planar rigid transform restores its heading and XY anchor. The initial tab is 11.970 mm above the bench, tilted 0.023°. The material and adhesive law are unchanged.

Convergence probes showed that changing friction smoothing was unnecessary and could degrade the chore. The final runtime restores the original 0.01 m/s friction-smoothing threshold and increases VBD iterations from 10 to 20. With the same settled start and standard friction, ten iterations with contact history still moved the centroid 1.716 mm in ten seconds (`run/warm_rest10.json`). Twenty iterations with history reduced this to 0.060 mm; at thirty seconds it was 0.113 mm, maximum particle 0.283 mm (`run/warm_rest.json`). Twenty iterations without history gave 0.057 mm centroid and 0.242 mm maximum-particle motion in ten seconds (`run/cold_rest20.json`). The final scene therefore uses twenty iterations and the normal cold contact solve, with no history or persistent-contact override. These probes identify numerical convergence of the coupled fold/tab/bench contacts as the remaining creep source.

The full scene is translated by (−0.6, +0.2, −0.728) metres to keep the thin label's coordinates near zero. Cameras, robot mounts, lights, table and room move together; the bench remains 728 mm above the floor and all relative geometry is preserved. The final solve remains 60 Hz with 16 substeps, compliant ALM, 100 MuJoCo iterations with 50 line-search iterations and the 1024 particle-contact buffer. `_bind_contact` still differs from the stock adapter only by excluding the parcel's non-colliding display mesh from box-shape validation. Full-scene rest and full-duration frozen measurements are recorded below.

**Rebuilding assets.** Asset-generating code is now at the top level: `art.py`, `make_parcel_mesh.py`, `make_workshop_assets.py`, `refine_wood.py`, and `settle_label.py`. **Motion and contact.** The pickup, carry, touchdown/lay/release and nine straight-down press lines retain the original plan. The approach now requests a 17.1 mm opening (0.82), which clears the parcel while leaving room around the original 8 mm tab. It closes gradually to a 0.93 request: 6.65 mm requested opening, measured at approximately 7.49 mm around the tab. The release still uses the wider 0.72 request. The right-arm panel uses the toolkit's native incremental Newton IK tracking solver, seeded from the physical arm's current joints. This is a local motion-planning choice, not a modified robot. The low pickup keeps its original wrist adjustment; after the lift and during pressing, the commanded wrist attitude stays fixed so cursor auto-seating cannot accumulate loaded wrist deflections.

The new, better-converged contact solve exposes a persistent swing of the light sheet during carry. `guided_touchdown` observes the free edge, averages its XY position over 36 control steps, and moves the hand with the swing to damp it while centring that average. It then lowers the hand until the free edge touches the box top, targeting the original label's free-end position 40 mm from the box centre. Only robot cursor commands change: there is no object pose write, attachment to the robot, altered adhesion or external force. The subsequent diagonal lay and release remain the original plan. This adds time to the hover; it also removes the sensitivity to the sheet's swing phase seen in fixed-waypoint trials.

Pressing opens the stock gripper fully (95 mm gap) and uses its positive-Y blade alone. The other blade stays outside the parcel. The cursor is offset by -49.9 mm in world Y, centring that blade's lower face on the intended press point; the collider's lower face is 16.5 mm below the tool point. These offsets come from the actual collider geometry (`run/fingertip_geometry.json`), not added or enlarged fingers. The measured full-open gap is 94.999 mm; the two lower blade faces span tool-frame Y 47.900–51.900 mm and -51.899–-47.899 mm. The original nine columns remain, now pressing every bondable 5 mm grid row. The 3.5 mm commanded press depth is retained. Inter-dab clearance is 10 mm instead of 6 mm, with a brief unloaded settle; each descent begins at the hand's actual XY to avoid rubbing during tracking lag. The held wrist target is cleared in `finally` even when a routine stops.

This is a visible change from the old nearly closed two-finger press. Trials with a small gap could catch the sheet, peel bonds and topple the tab; simply moving the endpoint inward was not sufficient in the stock-limit replay.
